Research

Attention can be subdivided into two sub-constructs known as internal and external attention. Internal attention refers to attention placed on memory representations, while external attention refers to sensory-based processes. Individuals consistently switch between these two attentional domains in order to accomplish goals throughout daily life. My research focuses on understanding attention mode switching in the following ways:

Neural/Behavioral Correlates of Attention Switching

My primary interest is in understanding the control mechanisms involved in attention switching using behavioral and neuroimaging methodologies.

Sensorimnemonic Decition Point

My secondary research line involves understanding how we choose whether to engage internal or external attention when we are met with a decision. How do individuals choose which attention mode to engage when presented with a choice.

The Relationship Between Internal and External Attention

My final line of research looks to understand the relationship between internal and external attention. Specifically, whether or not we are able to engage in both attention domains concurrently or are forced to engage in them in serial.
